Section 1 • Information Theory & Biostatistics

1. Information-Theoretic Rationale for 288-Point Continuous Ambulatory Sampling

Acute postprandial glycemia exhibits rapid absorption transients with rise times of 25–45 minutes and full excursion bandwidths spanning fmax ≈ 0.04–0.08 cycles/min (period ≥ 15 min). Measuring once per quarter (interval Δt = 129,600 min) or twice daily via pre-meal fingersticks grossly violates the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem, causing severe temporal aliasing that conceals +60 to +110 mg/dL food spikes and overnight nadir lows.[1][3][9] Continuous 5-minute wearable logging samples at fs = 0.2 cycles/min (Δt = 5 min), faithfully reconstructing postprandial velocity dG/dt, daytime nap episodes, and nocturnal recovery envelopes.[2][4]
