CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM & NOISE SUPPRESSION MATH

How sample size scales resolution and measurement quality: mathematical proof of SE = sigma / sqrt(N), Central Limit Theorem, and 161× SNR noise suppression.

  • More Samples = Higher Resolution & Quality: Metrological law dictates that measurement uncertainty is inversely proportional to the square root of sample size: SE = sigma / sqrt(N). Increasing N directly sharpens clinical resolution and suppresses stochastic noise.
  • Error Variance Reduction (Var = sigma^2 / N): N=2 quarterly lab draws yield an error variance of 0.5 sigma^2. LCARS N=25,920 reduces error variance to 0.000038 sigma^2—a 99.4% elimination of uncertainty, transforming noisy bio-signals into crystalline trajectories.
  • 161× Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR scales with sqrt(N)): Coherent continuous averaging enhances SNR by sqrtN. At N=25,920, random sensor noise and epidermal baseline wander are suppressed by a factor of 161 (24.1 dB SNR improvement), revealing subtle metabolic dynamics.
  • Autocorrelation & Temporal Repeatability: Discrete epochs enable continuous autocorrelation Rxx(τ) = E[x(t)x(t+τ)]. High-density time-series allow Kalman filtering and trend verification that sporadic spot-checks are mathematically incapable of performing.
