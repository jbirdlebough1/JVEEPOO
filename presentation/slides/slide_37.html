AMBULATORY PWTT TONOMETRY & DIPPING CLINICAL AUDIT
1. OPTICAL PULSE WAVE TRANSIT TIME (PWTT):
Derived from the Bramwell-Hill relationship: as arterial pressure rises, vascular walls stiffen, accelerating pulse wave propagation. Measuring transit time from the cardiac R-peak to the distal PPG pulse foot yields continuous systolic and diastolic trends without disruptive cuff inflations.
2. CLINICAL TARGET STANDARDS (AHA / ACC):
• Normotensive Target: <120 mmHg Systolic and <80 mmHg Diastolic.
• Hypertension Stage 1: 130–139 / 80–89 mmHg.
• Eliminates "White-Coat" anxiety spikes by evaluating 288 ambulatory measurements across routine daily activities.
3. NOCTURNAL DIPPING CLASSIFICATION:
• Normal Dippers (10%–20% Drop): Demonstrates healthy nocturnal parasympathetic dominance and vascular decompression.
• Non-Dippers (<10% Drop): Signals impaired autonomic regulation; strongly correlated with diabetic nephropathy and microalbuminuria.
• Reverse Dippers (Nocturnal Rise): 3.8× elevated hazard ratio for major adverse cardiac events.
DIABETIC CARDIOVASCULAR PROTECTION: Continuous nocturnal dipping monitoring empowers endocrinologists to schedule evening antihypertensive timing (e.g. ACE/ARB chrono-therapy) to restore protective dipping.
Key Takeaway: Delivers continuous ambulatory blood pressure curves and verifies protective nocturnal dipping.
